# Cargo Classification Guide

Every shipment must be declared under exactly one of the four cargo
classes below. The declared class determines which row of the rate table
applies — a misclassified shipment is charged (or credited) at a different
class's rate entirely, not a variation of the correct one.

## General Freight

The default classification. Palletized or containerized dry goods with no
special handling, temperature, or hazard requirements — packaged consumer
goods, dry industrial components, textiles, and similar cargo. If a shipment
does not clearly meet the criteria for perishable, hazardous, or oversized
below, it is general freight.

## Perishable Goods

Cargo requiring temperature-controlled or time-sensitive handling: fresh
produce, dairy, refrigerated or frozen goods, and pharmaceuticals with cold-
chain requirements. Perishable classification is what makes a shipment
eligible for the COLD-CHAIN exemption where applicable (see the exemptions
notice) — a shipment that should have been declared perishable but was
declared as general freight loses eligibility for that exemption in
addition to being charged at the wrong base rate.

## Hazardous Materials

Cargo subject to hazardous-materials handling requirements: flammable,
corrosive, toxic, or otherwise dangerous goods requiring placarding and
special handling under national transport safety rules. Hazardous cargo
always carries a surcharge — see the formula overview. Declaring hazardous
cargo as general freight to avoid the surcharge is a classification error,
regardless of intent.

## Oversized Loads

Cargo exceeding standard legal dimensions for the vehicle class carrying it,
requiring escort or route permitting. Oversized loads carry a surcharge
reflecting the additional road-safety burden. A shipment is oversized
because of its dimensions, independent of its weight or cargo contents —
an oversized general-freight shipment is still classified as oversized, not
general freight.
