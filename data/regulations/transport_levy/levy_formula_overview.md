# Transport Levy — Formula Overview

This note explains how the National Transport Levy Authority (NTLA)
calculates the levy owed on a freight shipment. It applies to every schedule
version; only the underlying rate figures change between versions (see the
version-specific rate notices).

## How the levy is calculated

The levy is the sum of three components, plus any applicable surcharge,
minus any applicable exemption discount:

1. **Base fee** — a flat charge determined by the shipment's cargo
   classification and vehicle classification.
2. **Distance charge** — the applicable per-kilometre rate multiplied by the
   declared trip distance.
3. **Weight charge** — the applicable per-kilogram rate multiplied by the
   portion of the declared weight that exceeds the free weight allowance for
   that cargo/vehicle combination. Weight at or below the allowance is not
   charged.

The base fee, distance charge, and weight charge are summed to a
pre-surcharge subtotal.

## Weight allowance

Every cargo/vehicle combination has its own free weight threshold. Only the
weight in excess of that threshold is billed at the per-kilogram rate — this
is not a discount, it is how the per-kilogram rate is defined. A shipment at
or under its threshold pays no weight charge at all, only the base fee and
distance charge.

## Surcharges

Certain cargo/vehicle combinations carry a surcharge, expressed as a
percentage applied to the pre-surcharge subtotal (base fee + distance charge
+ weight charge). Hazardous materials and oversized loads typically carry a
non-zero surcharge; general freight and perishable goods typically do not.
The surcharge is added to the subtotal before any exemption discount is
applied.

Exemption discounts, where applicable, are applied last — as a percentage of
the full subtotal including any surcharge. See the exemptions notice for
which codes exist and what they cover.
